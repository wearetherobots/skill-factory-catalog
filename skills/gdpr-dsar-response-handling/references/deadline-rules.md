# DSAR deadline rules

Open this at Step 3 and whenever the timeline changes.

## Base deadline (Art. 12(3))

- Respond "without undue delay and in any event within one month of receipt". One month is a backstop, not a target.
- Receipt = when the request reached the company through any channel or employee.
- EDPB Guidelines 01/2022 refer to Regulation (EEC, Euratom) No 1182/71 on periods, dates and time limits. Practical rule used by many authorities: the deadline is the same date in the following month; if that date does not exist, the last day of that month. Verify how the start day, weekends and public holidays are counted under the current guidelines and your supervisory authority's practice.

Set internal milestones: processor responses due at T-14 days, draft complete T-7, sign-off T-3.

## Identity verification and clarification

- Whether the clock stops while waiting for ID or clarification: verify the current EDPB position and national practice. Conservative default: request ID or clarification on day 1-3 and keep working on the search in parallel.
- Clarification: where you process a large quantity of information, Recital 63 allows you to ask the person to specify the information or processing the request relates to. They may decline; you must then still respond fully. Do not use clarification as a delaying tactic.

## Extension (Art. 12(3))

- Up to two further months, "where necessary, taking into account the complexity and number of the requests".
- You must inform the requester of the extension within one month of receipt, together with the reasons for the delay.
- Valid reasons (document them): very large volumes across many systems; multiple related requests from the same person; need to consult many third parties about their data. Weak reasons: staff holiday, backlog, poor data mapping.
- Even when extending, send what is ready.

Extension notice wording:

> We received your access request on [date]. Because [specific reason, e.g. your data is held across 14 systems including archived support records, and your request is one of three related requests you have made], we need more time and will respond by [date, max. two further months]. [We enclose the information we can already provide.]

## Manifestly unfounded or excessive (Art. 12(5))

- Only in clear cases. Burden of proof is on the company. Wanting the data for a dispute is not by itself abusive; the first copy is free regardless of motive (CJEU C-307/22).
- Possible indicators of excessive: repetitive requests at short intervals without new processing (Recital 63 refers to exercising the right "at reasonable intervals"). Indicators of unfounded: request explicitly made to cause disruption, or offering to withdraw in exchange for a benefit. Escalate to DPO before relying on either.
- Options: charge a reasonable fee based on administrative costs, or refuse to act.
- If refusing or not acting: inform within one month of receipt of the reasons and of the right to lodge a complaint with a supervisory authority and to seek a judicial remedy (Art. 12(4)).

## Fees

- First copy: free (Art. 15(3)).
- Further copies: a reasonable fee based on administrative costs may be charged (Art. 15(3)). Verify any national rules.

## Log every date

Received, acknowledged, ID requested, ID confirmed, processor requests sent/returned, extension notice sent, response sent. These dates are your evidence if a complaint follows.
