# Risks and asks

Open during Step 7.

## Risk categories to scan

Prompt the founder on each category; include only the top 3-5 that are real this quarter.

- Cash and financing: runway under the time a raise typically takes, burn rising faster than the core metric, dependency on an unclosed bridge or SAFE extension, venture debt covenants.
- Customer concentration: one customer above ~10% of revenue, renewal or renegotiation pending, a design partner who may not convert.
- Growth engine: pipeline coverage below target, CAC rising, a channel that worked last quarter now saturating, activation or retention cohorts weakening.
- Product and technical: key launch slipped, reliability incidents, security or data-privacy exposure, dependency on a single platform/API vendor.
- Team: key-person dependency (often a founder or the only engineer on a core system), co-founder friction, hiring for a critical role behind schedule, attrition.
- Market and competition: well-funded entrant, pricing pressure, platform policy change.
- Regulatory and legal: sector rules (health, financial services, data protection), disputes, IP ownership gaps. Describe the exposure; the advice belongs to counsel.

For each risk write: Likelihood (L/M/H), Impact (L/M/H), Mitigation already underway (not planned intentions only), Owner (a named person), Escalate if (an observable trigger with a number or date).

Bad: "Competition is increasing."
Good: "[Competitor] raised a large round and is discounting 40% against us in 2 of 9 open deals. Likelihood M / Impact M. Mitigation: annual-prepay discount tested in 3 deals. Owner: CEO. Escalate if we lose 2 more deals on price this quarter."

Include at least one risk the board may not already know. If the founder resists putting a sensitive item (co-founder conflict, pending churn, legal claim) in writing, note in "Questions for founder" that it should at least be raised live in the meeting.

## Writing asks

A board ask works when a director can act on it within a week without a follow-up question.

Each ask needs:
1. Type - Intro, Hiring, Advice, or Decision.
2. Specific target - named companies, role profile, or the exact question.
3. Why it matters - one clause linking it to a goal or risk.
4. Who - the director best placed, if known.
5. By when.

Limit to 5, ranked by impact. Drop vague asks rather than pad the list.

Bad: "Any help with fundraising would be appreciated."
Good: "Intro: 3 Series A partners who led vertical SaaS rounds in the last 18 months - [lead investor director] - by Dec 15, so we can run first meetings in January."

Bad: "Help with hiring."
Good: "Hiring: 2 referrals for a VP Sales who has taken a mid-market SaaS team from roughly $0.5M to $3M ARR; we will interview in December."

Bad: "Thoughts on pricing?"
Good: "Advice: should we move from per-seat to usage-based pricing for new deals in Q1? Options and data in Appendix E. Want a view in the meeting."

## Decisions vs. discussions

Separate items the board must decide from items where the founder wants input.

- Decision asks: state the options, the founder's recommendation, and the deadline.
- If the decision normally requires formal board or shareholder approval under the company's charter, investor agreements or local corporate law (commonly: new financings or SAFEs, option grants and pool increases, annual budget, officer appointments, large contracts, debt), label it "Formal approval needed - counsel to prepare consent/resolution". Do not draft the resolution and do not state which approvals are legally required; verify with company counsel.

## Tone checks before finalizing

- Is the worst news in the first half of page one?
- Does every miss have a cause and a change?
- Would a director know what to do after reading the asks?
- Remove: "crushing it", "exciting", "headwinds" without specifics, unexplained acronyms, metrics with no comparison.
